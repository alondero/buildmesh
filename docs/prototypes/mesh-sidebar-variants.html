PROTOTYPEA — Tidy Current

Mock data: 4 meshes, 7 agents, 1 waiting on you, 1 failed, 1 mesh fault (infra ↓3 + sync failed). Read-only; hover rows to reveal hidden actions. Flip variants below or with ← →.

working needs you failed ready suspended idle / starting

What changed vs today

  • One fault language: drift, behind-count, and sync-failure collapse into at most two pills per mesh; the ! always means “click to fix in Worktrees”, never decoration.
  • Hover-only chrome: delete, retry, drag handle hide until hover; status dot + name + provider carry the row at rest.
  • Attention keeps its border: only “needs you” and “failed” rows get tinted fills; everything else drops the mesh-colour wash.

Kept / dropped

  • Kept: mesh tree, Active grouping, per-mesh + New, provider tags, counts.
  • Dropped from rest state: drag glyphs, sync icon (failure-only pill instead), restart affordances on healthy rows.

B — Quiet Rows

  • Text over glyphs: status is a plain word (running, waiting on you, failed); the dot is 8px and decorative.
  • No mesh colours at all — identity comes from the name and the header count line, not washes or accent bars.
  • Spawn on hover only, so an idle sidebar reads as a calm list, not a wall of buttons.

Trade-off

  • Scans slower for “which mesh is hot?” across 10+ meshes; wins on visual noise. Best if you mostly live in 2–4 meshes.

C — Triage Sections

  • Attention is the hierarchy: Needs you / Working / Quiet replace the mesh tree as the top split; mesh becomes a second-line context.
  • One spawn entry point at the top (“New agent…”) instead of a + on every mesh.
  • Mesh health gets its own shelf at the bottom so infra faults can’t hide inside a collapsed mesh.

Trade-off

  • Fastest “what do I owe the machine?” read; weakest “where does this agent live?” intuition. Pairs well with the canvas grid for spatial context.

D — Mesh Cards

  • Each mesh is a card with its own header (dot, name, count, +) — scannable at 6–8 meshes, roomy click targets.
  • Faults promoted to card chrome: the infra card carries the ↓3 + sync-failed pills in its header and an inline “fix sync” line.
  • Action rows only when actionable: Open / Retry buttons appear solely on needs-you and failed nodes.

Trade-off

  • Uses ~30% more vertical space than A/B; the calmest to click through with a trackpad.

E — Activity Feed (radical)

  • No tree at all: one time-ordered feed; mesh names shrink to grey context text.
  • “What should happen next?” card owns the only two primary buttons (reply, retry) — everything else is read-only narrative.
  • Quiet agents collapse to one line at the bottom; health events (drift) are feed entries, not badges.

Trade-off

  • Most “modern agent inbox” feeling; loses drag-reorder, per-mesh spawn, and the spatial map of where work lives. A real departure — needs the canvas to carry structure.

F — Compact Dots (radical)

  • One line per mesh: stacked status dots + count tell you “3 agents, one needs you” without listing anyone.
  • Expand to work: disclosure rows reveal nodes; faults (↓3, !) live on the collapsed line so nothing hides.
  • Densest option: ~12 meshes fit where today fits 5; hover/expand carries every action.

Trade-off

  • Maximum density, minimum glanceability into names. Built for operators juggling many meshes who navigate by mesh, not by node.

G — Colour-bar compact (round-2 baseline)

  • Exactly your brief: F’s one-line rows plus A’s colour bar. Click expands; faults stay on the collapsed line.
  • Judge H–K against this. If none beats it, G is the build.

H — Hover peek

  • Hover expands, click pins: zero clicks to scan every mesh; pinned rows survive cursor travel; keyboard focus behaves like hover.
  • Risk: rows shift under the cursor and accidental brushes fire expansion — trackpad-heavy users may hate it. A small open delay would be the production tuning knob.

I — Headline compact

  • The collapsed line names the one thing: “probe-tabs needs you”, “login-screen failed”, “all quiet”. You expand only to act, not to understand.
  • Cost: a second text line per mesh and a headline rule to design (most severe fault wins; ties broken by recency).

J — Signal bar

  • Dots become one proportional strip: a sliver of amber/red shouts from the collapsed line; an all-cyan bar reads healthy without reading anything.
  • Cost: the segment→status mapping must be learned (legend provided); thin segments are hard to hit as anything but signal.

K — Attention-led

  • Hot meshes open themselves (“▾ auto”); quiet ones sleep at one dimmed line. Triage in one glance, then “Collapse all” resets the board.
  • Risk: the sidebar rearranges itself as statuses flip — spatial memory suffers. Best if attention flips are infrequent, worst in a very lively session.

L — Bordered compact (finalist, the safe build)

  • G plus F’s enclosure: header and nodes share one card border, split by a hairline. Click to expand, faults on the collapsed line.
  • No hover behaviour at all — nothing moves unless you click it. If M and N lose, L ships.

M — Hover headline (finalist)

  • Hover shows a floating headline bubble (“probe-tabs needs you · 2 more working”); it overlays the list, so rows never shift and your tunnel scenario cannot happen.
  • Click still opens the card. Trade-off: the headline rule (most severe fault wins) needs designing, and the bubble briefly covers the row below.

N — Dwell-and-grace hover (finalist, try sweeping the list)

  • 200ms dwell ignores passing brushes; 350ms grace survives cursor travel; sliding onto a neighbour transfers the peek (120ms) instead of collapsing it. Click pins.
  • Trade-off: the most engineered option — three timing knobs to tune, and constant expand/collapse motion for users who mostly navigate by click (L people).

What today is telling you (audit)

  • Must know at a glance: mesh identity, node name, working vs waiting-on-you vs broken, and how many agents each mesh holds.
  • Should know on demand: provider, pairing, drift/behind/sync faults with a fix path, retry/resume on failed rows.
  • Noise candidates: permanent drag handles, colour washes on every row, provider icons on every row, sync icon at rest, restart buttons on healthy rows, dual Workspace/Attention tabs plus an Active label.
